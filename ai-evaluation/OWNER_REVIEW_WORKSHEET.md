## Record a defensible decision

1. Read the source and instruction before comparing the response against a reference key. Existing worked examples may already be familiar, so reviewing those is **not** a genuinely blind test.
2. For single responses, choose **ACCEPT** or **REJECT** and rate applicable rubric dimensions. Separately note if the underlying fact cannot be established.
3. For pairs, choose **A**, **B**, **TIE**, or **NEITHER**, and explain the decisive difference or why the pair is too close to distinguish.
4. Cite the precise source text, numerical calculation, formatting requirement, or disclosure rule that determined the result.
5. For Spanish-source or Spanish-output work, write at least **two original explanations in Spanish**, covering register, regional meaning, nuance or conditional statements. Don't count a translation that only swaps obvious opposite words as a difficult judgment.
6. Record the actual date and your first assessment. If you consult an AI-assisted reference afterward, retain your original response and describe any disagreement. Never fill in a review, date or second reviewer that did not exist.

## What a recruiter should be able to inspect once you finish

For one completed case, show the supplied source and instruction, the response exactly as it was evaluated, your initial verdict and dimension ratings, and a three- or four-sentence explanation. For a Spanish example, include the original wording and your own Spanish rationale. Preserve corrections and disagreements rather than editing the original decision away.

**Do not publish a reference-agreement percentage as evaluator accuracy.** Comparisons against AI-assisted labels are useful as practice, not a calibrated human or model benchmark.
